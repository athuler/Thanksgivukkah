Thanksgivukkah FAQ

What is Thanksgivukkah?

Thanksgivukkah is the rare day when American Thanksgiving falls during Hanukkah, the eight-night Jewish Festival of Lights. The name blends "Thanksgiving" and "Hanukkah" and became popular across the U.S. in 2013.

Source: Wikipedia, Thanksgivukkah

When is the next Thanksgivukkah?

The next Thanksgivukkah is Thursday, November 27, 2070. The first candle of Hanukkah is lit on the evening of Thanksgiving Day.

Source: Wikipedia, Thanksgivukkah

When was the last Thanksgivukkah?

When will Thanksgivukkah happen after 2070?

After 2070 the next one is Thursday, November 28, 2165, again on the evening of Thanksgiving. After that the calendars drift apart: by this site's calculation, Thanksgiving next falls during Hanukkah in the year 76942. A widely cited estimate puts the next time the first day of Hanukkah lands on Thanksgiving in the year 79811.

Source: Wikipedia, Thanksgivukkah

Has Thanksgivukkah happened before 2013?

Yes. Since the first national Thanksgiving in 1863, Thanksgiving also fell during Hanukkah in 1888 (the first day of Hanukkah), 1899 (the fourth day) and 1918.

Source: Wikipedia, Thanksgivukkah

How often does Thanksgivukkah happen?

Very rarely: six times between 1863 and 2165, and then not for tens of thousands of years. Most people will see it at most once in their lifetime.

Source: Wikipedia, Thanksgivukkah

Why is Thanksgivukkah so rare?

Thanksgiving follows the Gregorian calendar and always falls between November 22 and 28. Hanukkah starts on 25 Kislev in the lunisolar Hebrew calendar, so its Gregorian date moves from year to year, anywhere from late November to early January. The two calendars have slightly different average year lengths, so the Hebrew calendar drifts about one day every 216 years, slowly moving Hanukkah away from late November.

Sources: Wikipedia, Thanksgivukkah; Wikipedia, Hebrew calendar

When is Thanksgiving?

In the United States, Thanksgiving is the fourth Thursday of November. From Lincoln's 1863 proclamation it was held on the last Thursday of November. In 1939 President Roosevelt moved it to the second-to-last Thursday, and on December 26, 1941 Congress fixed it as the fourth Thursday.

Source: U.S. National Archives, Congress Establishes Thanksgiving

When does Hanukkah start?

Hanukkah begins on 25 Kislev in the Hebrew calendar and lasts eight nights and days. Hebrew calendar days begin at sunset, so the first candle of the menorah is lit on the evening before.

Source: Wikipedia, Hanukkah

What does the countdown count down to?

It counts down to the moment Thanksgiving and Hanukkah overlap. In 2070 that is the first candle lighting on Thanksgiving evening, set at 4:30 pm US Eastern time, around sundown.
